Methods: Talinolol PBPK model: fit PK

Parameters and data

8 parameters were optimized: tissue/plasma partition coefficient tal (Kp_tal), tissue distribution tal (ftissue_tal), renal clearance of unbound talinolol relative to the GFR (KI__TALEX_f), Vmax for talinolol import (LI__TALIM_Vmax), Ka_dis [1/hr] dissolution tal (Ka_dis_tal), rate of the apical uptake of talinolol (GU__TALABS_k), Vmax of the efflux of talinolol by P-glycoprotein (GU__TALEFL_Vmax) and Km of the efflux of talinolol by P-glycoprotein (GU__TALEFL_Km).

A fit mapping is a curve of a study, the data and the observable of the model it is compared with. The fit has 85 fit mappings of training data with 874 data points, whose residuals enter the objective; 64 fit mappings of validation data with 756 data points, which were simulated and evaluated with the fitted parameters but not fitted; and 12 fit mappings of outliers with 90 data points, data the fit dropped as not usable, which were evaluated like the validation data so that the decision to drop them can be checked. The fit was set up and run with sbmlsim [1].

Objective function

The parameters were estimated by minimizing the objective function

F(p→)=12∑k∑iρ(wi,kri,k(p→)2)(1)

where k runs over the fit mappings of the training data and i over their data points, ri,k is the residual, wi,k=wkwi,kp the weight of the data point and ρ the loss function, applied to the squared weighted residual z=wi,kri,k2. The loss function is linear, ρ(z)=z, i.e. a standard least squares problem.

The residuals are normalized by the mean y―k of the data of their curve, which makes curves of different quantities and magnitudes comparable:

ri,k=yi,k−fk(xi,k,p→)y―k(2)

The weight of curve k is wk=nk/Nk, the weight nk of its fit mapping (the weight its fit mapping collection assigns, 1 by default, or, for a collection which uses the weights of its mappings, the weight of the mapping, by default the number of subjects of its data) divided by the number Nk of its data points.

A data point is weighted with the inverse of its coefficient of variation, wi,kp=|yi,k|/σi,k, with σi,k the standard deviation of the data point, or its standard error for data without a standard deviation. An error of zero is missing, a missing error of a curve with errors is replaced by the largest error of the curve, and the data points of a curve without errors have the weight wi,kp=2, the weight of a coefficient of variation of 0.5.

Optimization

The optimizer searched the parameters in log10 space within their bounds; bounds, values and intervals are given in the units of the fit parameters, the units of the table of the parameters.

The objective was minimized in 3 runs with differential evolution [2] of SciPy [3] within the bounds of the parameters, with the strategy best1bin, a population of 15 times the number of parameters, at most 1000 generations, the relative tolerance 0.01 of convergence, the recombination constant 0.7 and a mutation dithered between 0.5 and 1. The initial population of a run was drawn by latin hypercube sampling [4], with a seed of every run derived from the seed 1234 of the fit. The best member of the final population was polished with the L-BFGS-B method of SciPy.

The convergence of the runs was assessed from the traces of their cost and the waterfall plot of their final costs, and the parameter set with the lowest cost was used.

The models were simulated with libRoadRunner [5] and its integrator CVODE [6] with the relative tolerance 10−6 and the absolute tolerances 10−12 of an amount, 10−12 of a concentration and 10−6 of the other states, which for a species in a concentration is multiplied by the initial volume of its compartment, and 1 state of its own. It ran with the variable step size of libRoadRunner, i.e. with an output at every step of the integrator. A data point at the time of a change after the start of a simulation, e.g. a dose or the reset of a collection, was compared with the state before the change, except the first data point of a curve with data at later times, which starts a collection and was compared with the state after the change.

Goodness of fit

The goodness of fit is given per kind of data (training data, validation data and outliers) by metrics over its n data points with the residuals ei=yi−f(xi,p→^) at the fitted parameters p→^, the mean squared error, its root and the root mean squared error normalized by the mean y―k(i) of the data of the curve of a point:

MSE=1n∑i=1nei2,RMSE=MSE,NRMSE=1n∑i=1n(eiy―k(i))2(3)

The root mean square of the residuals of the objective, i.e. what the fit minimizes, and the coefficient of determination:

RMSEw=1n∑i=1nρ(wiri2),R2=1−∑iei2∑i(yi−y―)2(4)

The predictions of a non-linear model are no linear regression of the data, so R2 can be negative: the prediction is worse than the mean of the data. The information criteria of Akaike [7] and Schwarz [8] with the number np of fitted parameters are those of a least squares fit with normally distributed residuals, up to an additive constant, so only differences between models fitted to the same data are meaningful:

AIC=nln(MSE)+2np,BIC=nln(MSE)+npln(n)(5)

The percentage of the data points whose prediction fi=f(xi,p→^) lies within twofold of the data is taken over the n+ data points with positive data yi>0 and a positive prediction fi>0; the others have no ratio and are not counted, and a kind of data without such a point has no percentage:

P2=100n+|{i:12≤fiyi≤2}|(6)

The goodness of fit plot shows the predictions against the data on logarithmic axes, with the lines of twofold f=2y and f=y/2. The Bland-Altman plot [9] shows the ratio of prediction and data as di=log10(fi/yi) against the geometric mean fiyi for the data points with positive values, with the bias d― and the limits of agreement d―±1.96sd, with the sample standard deviation sd of di, of the training data; they are read as the fold factors 10d― and 10d―±1.96sd.

Identifiability

The identifiability of the parameters was analysed with the profile likelihood [10, 11]. The profile of a parameter is the lowest objective with the parameter fixed:

PL(pj)=minpl≠jF(p→)(7)

At every point of a scan the other parameters were re-optimized from the previous point with the least squares optimizer of SciPy with the relative step 0.05. A scan ran from the optimum in both directions in the space of the optimizer, with steps in decades of the parameter, starting at 0.1. A step which raised the objective by more than 0.2 of the distance between the objective at the optimum and the threshold was reduced by the factor 2 and repeated, down to 0.01, and the step after one which raised it by less than that divided by the factor was enlarged by the factor, up to 1. A scan stopped at the threshold, at the bound of the parameter, at a point which failed or after 50 points.

The confidence intervals are the values at which the profile crosses the threshold of the likelihood ratio test

Fthr=Fmin+χα2(df)2(8)

with Fmin the lowest objective of the fit and of all profiles and χα2(df) the quantile of the χ2 distribution at the confidence level α=0.95 with df = 1 degrees of freedom, i.e. 1.92 above the lowest objective. The threshold has this confidence level if F is the negative log-likelihood of the data up to a constant, i.e. if the weighted residuals are independent and normally distributed with unit variance; otherwise the level is approximate. A crossing is interpolated between two points of the profile, on the logarithm of a parameter on a logarithmic scale. A parameter is identifiable if its profile reaches the threshold within the scan on both sides of the optimum, i.e. its confidence interval is finite; practically non-identifiable towards the lower bound if its profile reaches the threshold above the optimum but not below it within the scan, which ends at the lower bound or after 50 points; practically non-identifiable towards the upper bound if its profile reaches the threshold below the optimum but not above it within the scan, which ends at the upper bound or after 50 points; practically non-identifiable if its profile reaches the threshold on neither side within the scans, which end at the bounds or after 50 points, and is not flat; and structurally non-identifiable if its profile is flat on both sides. A side of a profile is flat if it rises by less than 0.05 of the distance between the lowest objective and the threshold, and a profile with a point which failed or did not converge is not classified. The profile likelihood is preferred over the intervals of the Fisher information, its intervals are invariant under a transformation of the parameters and can be asymmetric [12].

The Fisher information is the local alternative to the profiles: it is the Gauss-Newton approximation of the curvature of the objective at the optimum, from the Jacobian J of the weighted residuals of the training data with respect to the parameters p→ in the space of the optimizer, computed by central finite differences with the step hj=ϵmax(|pj|,1), ϵ=10−6 of the parameter pj:

I=J⊤J,cov=σ2I−1,σ2=2Fn−np(9)

with the number n of data points of the training data and the number np of parameters. The information has rank 8 of 8, the number of its eigenvalues above 10−8 of the largest one, and the condition number 1950; the covariance of an information of a lower rank is computed with the pseudo-inverse. The Wald intervals are p^j±t(1+α)/2,n−npSEj with the standard errors SEj=covjj at the confidence level α=0.95, transformed back into the units of the fit parameters.

Fisher information

The curvature of the cost at the parameters, FIM = J'J with the jacobian of the weighted residuals, from which the standard errors and the correlations of the parameters follow. It is a local statement: the intervals are exact for a cost which is a quadratic around the optimum and approximate for a non-linear model, where the profile likelihood is the one to trust. The errors and the intervals are computed in the space the optimizer searches, so an interval on a logarithmic scale is not symmetric around the value.

Profile likelihood

Profile likelihood of the parameters: every parameter is fixed at values around the optimum and the other parameters are optimized again. A parameter is identifiable if its profile crosses the threshold of the confidence level on both sides of the optimum, i.e., its confidence interval is finite; practically non-identifiable if the profile stays below the threshold up to a bound of the parameter; structurally non-identifiable if the profile is flat. An open side of an interval is shown as the bound of the parameter.

A profile found a cost below the cost of the parameter set, the fit did not converge to the optimum. The threshold is relative to the lowest cost.
